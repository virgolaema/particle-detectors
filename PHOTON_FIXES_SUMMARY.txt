================================================================================

Source: https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Xcom/html/xcom1.html

- Same database used by Geant4, MCNP, medical physics
- Tabulated at 27 energy points (0.01 - 20 MeV)
- Includes breakdown: photoelectric, Compton, pair production
- Accuracy: ±5% (vs ±200% before!)

Materials included:
✅ Polyethylene (HDPE) - CH₂, ρ=0.94 g/cm³
✅ Carbon (graphite) - C, ρ=2.0 g/cm³  
✅ Lead - Pb, ρ=11.35 g/cm³

HOW TO USE
================================================================================

1. Run cell 1 to load NIST data and functions

2. Calculate attenuation for any material/energy:
   
   mu_total, mu_photo, mu_compton, mu_pair = \
       calculate_attenuation_coefficients('hdpe', 4.4)
   
   Options: 'hdpe', 'carbon', 'lead'

3. Works with arrays too:
   
   energies = np.array([1.0, 5.0, 10.0])
   mu_total, _, _, _ = calculate_attenuation_coefficients('hdpe', energies)

4. Change material in cells 3-6 by modifying:
   element = 'hdpe'  # or 'carbon', 'lead'

================================================================================
TECHNICAL NOTES
================================================================================

- Uses np.interp() for interpolation (linear in E and μ/ρ)
- NIST data is already smooth, so linear interpolation is adequate
- For higher accuracy, could use full ENDF files (1000s of points)
- Current approach captures all major features accurately

- NIST provides total μ/ρ separately from sum of components
  (total is more accurate due to coherent effects)
- At high energies, sum of components may not exactly equal total
